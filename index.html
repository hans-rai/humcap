A
Suggested Action A
Country Level
In the next CPD, to align with the global humanitarian strategy and to effectively operationalize UNFPA's humanitarian mandate, a dedicated output on humanitarian action is critical. UNFPA Bangladesh should also provide explicit reference to the core elements of working at the nexus in the CPD.

Linking to conclusions 1, 4
Conclusion 1 ▼ Conclusion 4 ▼
"UNFPA in Bangladesh is committed to investing in climate adaptation and working across the nexus, and is spearheading many important initiatives that facilitate preparedness, early disaster response, resilient early recovery, and, in Cox's Bazar, area-based responses that include host communities. The current CPD underscores this commitment, as does UNFPA's innovative programming such as cash and voucher assistance, anticipatory action and decentralized procurement. However, there is no specific articulation in the CPD or in specific country programme guidance on what the nexus entails for the CO."
"UNFPA support has led to significant increases in utilization of a range of SRH, GBV and A&Y services in Cox's Bazar and to a lesser, but still important, extent in sudden-onset disasters (where the primary responsibility for service delivery lies with the GoB) over the evaluation period. UNFPA has supported improvement in the quality of these services over time to better address the needs of specific populations, such as PWD and third gender populations. Moreover, UNFPA has sought to maximize efficiency in delivery of services through integrated approaches. However, despite UNFPA's strong relationship with the Government of Bangladesh and their endorsement of the criticality of UNFPA mandated areas in humanitarian response, ensuring SRH, GBV and A&Y services in humanitarian response presents ongoing and future challenges, particularly in relation to accelerating funding shortfalls."
Supporting Findings
11 findings
1
EQ1: Relevance/Appropriateness
While the current (10th) CPD for Bangladesh (2022-2026) departs from the previous CPD's humanitarian commitments insofar as it does not reference refugee response, there is significantly increased attention to disaster preparedness and response and UNFPA's commitment to working across the humanitarian-development continuum in the 10th CPD.
Rationale & Evidence
CPD Context:
The 10th CPD explicitly excludes commitments directly related to the Rohingya crisis. While there is no specific humanitarian output in the 10th CPD, there is explicit recognition throughout of Bangladesh's high risk of exposure to climate-induced shocks.
Key Evidence:
"Several key informants from UNFPA expressed concern that the meaning of the humanitarian-development continuum is not specifically defined within the 10th CPD document, nor elsewhere in country guidance. As a result, even if UNFPA key informants have largely 'embraced a mindset of working at the nexus,' several interviewees from UNFPA felt it was not always clear what specific targets of this work are (or should be) in Bangladesh."
4
EQ2: SRH Effectiveness
UNFPA's programming in Bangladesh significantly aims to provide quality SRH services and commodities in humanitarian response, with efforts focused on accessibility and the specific needs of vulnerable populations. However, the extent to which these services consistently meet quality and accessibility standards across all affected areas varies.
Rationale & Evidence
SRH Commodities & Services:
UNFPA covers almost 90% of the SRH commodities across the response in Cox's Bazar. The SRH Working Group started to take targeted actions to improve service quality through its Quality Improvement Technical Team, led by UNFPA's Quality Assurance Analyst.
Key Evidence:
"According to several key informants external to UNFPA, the agency has worked well with the government to promote the government's Family Planning Strategy, while also supporting the rights, choices, dignity and needs of refugee women and girls in relation to SRH services." One external key informant stated, "I think UNFPA has done an excellent job at managing those sensitivities and political dynamics in a difficult policy context."
5
EQ2: SRH Effectiveness
Utilization of a broad range of quality SRH services has improved in Cox's Bazar and to a lesser (but still important extent) in acute disasters during the review period.
Rationale & Evidence
Results:
Contraceptive prevalence rates increased from almost 33% in 2021 to 55% in 2024 among the Rohingyas. Adolescent pregnancies fell from 18% in 2019 to 7% in 2024. Maternal deaths declined from 84 in 2021 to 46 in 2024.
Key Evidence:
"Data from the SRH response to flash floods in 2022 suggests that a total of 1,213 safe deliveries were conducted by skilled midwives during the 9 months of the project period--a significant increase compared to only 120 deliveries in the same facilities in the 9 months prior to the intervention."
6
EQ3: GBV Effectiveness
UNFPA and partners have made notable and ongoing contributions to the quality and effectiveness of GBV response in Bangladesh's humanitarian crises.
Rationale & Evidence
GBV Operations:
UNFPA has led the GBV Sub-Sector since 2017, supporting delivery of GBV services through 21 UNFPA-supported health facilities and 55 women-friendly spaces across 33 camps in Cox's Bazar. At the national level for disaster response, UNFPA co-leads the national GBV Cluster with MoWCA.
Funding Impact:
"The most recent GBVIMS (Q1 2025) Bulletin notes that the US Government funding freeze had a profound effect on GBV actors, leading to disruptions in service delivery across eight camps and two host communities: Women and Girls' Safe Spaces (WGSS) in Camps 5, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 22, 25, and other GBV service points were either closed or operated on reduced schedules."
7
EQ3: GBV Effectiveness
Uptake of GBV services in humanitarian crises in Bangladesh has improved, particularly in the refugee response, but also for disaster-affected populations. However, data on the impact of services on survivors, their families and communities are lacking.
Rationale & Evidence
Uptake Data:
In 2022, UNFPA-supported WFS' addressed the needs of over 311,000 women and girls; in 2023, more than 326,000 accessed GBV prevention and response services through WFS. Willingness to report GBV increased from 14% in 2018 to 85% in 2024.
Impact Data Gap:
"IPs more often report on activities and numbers reached, rather than behavioral change or other impact indicators, such as women's decreased risk of violence. The absence of baseline or endline evaluations further inhibits the ability of UNFPA and its IPs to measure change."
8
EQ4: Data for Response
UNFPA is facilitating improved collection, use and dissemination of reliable programme and population data in humanitarian response in Bangladesh, although UNFPA could improve its ability to show how its humanitarian support is lifesaving for women and girls (particularly in the area of GBV), and generates important positive impacts for communities more broadly.
Rationale & Evidence
Data Systems:
UNFPA regularly collects data disaggregated by age, sex, location, PwD, type of beneficiaries and services. UNFPA utilizes monitoring systems including analyzing real-time data on service uptake to identify areas with low uptake and make quick programmatic adjustments.
Key Evidence:
"The current tracking system for humanitarian response uses basic tools like google sheets to collect data, and a free version of KOBO collect, which is a challenge as this version limits how much data can be collected." The lack of dedicated human resources for M&E for disaster response is another challenge.
11
EQ6: Efficiency
UNFPA Bangladesh has implemented several procedural improvements and innovations to facilitate humanitarian response, with key support from the UNFPA AP regional office.
Rationale & Evidence
SOPs Achievement:
A significant achievement was the finalization in 2023 of country-level SOPs. According to a UNFPA key informant, their implementation has reduced response time significantly, from nearly three months after a disaster to three days. In 2024, UNFPA Bangladesh finalized an Anticipatory Action protocol.
Other Innovations:
"The UNFPA CO has participated in and/or introduced a number of other innovations, such as those mentioned previously: AA, and the use of cash and voucher assistance leveraging blockchain technology for security." Several UNFPA key informants noted how useful the regional office has been in facilitating learning and providing support.
13
EQ6: Efficiency
Although well-staffed in comparison to other UNFPA COs, ensuring adequate human resources has been a challenge in both Cox's Bazar and in UNFPA's disaster response.
Rationale & Evidence
HR Challenges:
In the past there have been leadership vacuums and severe staffing reductions in key areas like GBV coordination, impacting agency presence, strength and advocacy capacity. Several UNFPA key informants felt the scope and frequency of disasters in Bangladesh require a more substantial team of humanitarian-dedicated staff.
Key Evidence:
"A human resources realignment took place around mid-2023, of which the primary goal was to transition a large number of Cox's Bazar staff to fixed-term contracts (FTA) instead of temporary appointments or service contracts. UNFPA Cox's Bazar managed to complete a 'remarkable' 40 recruitments in one year as part of this realignment."
14
EQ7: Coherence
UNFPA has mainstreamed integration of GBV, SRH and youth services as a standard approach in both refugee response and disasters to build synergies and maximize resources.
Rationale & Evidence
Integration Examples:
GBV case workers are placed in SRH facilities. SRH midwives are placed in WFS. All UNFPA's centers and facilities generally provide a service for referral to its other programmes. Mobile SRH Health Camps provide referrals for GBV response services.
Key Evidence:
"UNFPA documentation regularly emphasizes the benefits of this integration, in terms of ensuring needs of women and girls are met more comprehensively; programmes are mutually supportive; and entry points for women and girls to access services are amplified." Between 2018 and 2024 adolescent pregnancies fell from over 18% to 7%.
16
EQ8: Connectedness
UNFPA promotes several humanitarian preparedness and response interventions in Bangladesh that support, and plan for, longer-term developmental and resilience-related goals.
Rationale & Evidence
HDP Nexus Programming:
UNFPA implements initiatives integrated with the HDP nexus including conditional cash support for adolescent girls to stay in school after disasters to prevent child marriage. UNFPA has also drawn from its emergency work—such as support to the national hotline in emergency response—to build out the hotline response in development.
Key Evidence:
"UNFPA has engaged in discussions with and received funding from International Financial Institutions such as the World Bank and Asian Development Bank, recognizing the potential for development funding to support resilience activities for both host and refugee populations."
17
EQ8: Connectedness
While UNFPA's humanitarian interventions often include capacity development and ownership at national and local levels, short-term and limited funding affects success in this area.
Rationale & Evidence
Sustainability Challenge:
A key example of government ownership is the integration of the MISP into the government operational plan. UNFPA trains midwives in collaboration with the government and deploys them to government facilities, making the humanitarian response "just integrated part of the government system." UNFPA explicitly mentions granting 83% of its allocation to national SRH partners in one project.
Key Evidence:
"The counselor who deployed to the government facility, I would say that is not fully sustainable, because whenever any disaster ends they lose funding. But there is some progress, like in our eastern flood, the understanding about the importance of MISP was not there among the government, but through our humanitarian response, at least now, they understand the importance." – UNFPA Key Informant
B
Suggested Action B
Country Level
UNFPA should continue its innovative work to explore different funding streams for emergencies, including through anticipatory action and engagement with IFIs. As part of this, UNFPA must be able to illustrate clearly the life-saving nature of its work (see recommendation C). UNFPA Bangladesh should document successes for wider agency learning.

Linking to conclusion 2
Conclusion 2 ▼
"UNFPA is widely recognized as a key UN partner in the Rohingya refugee response and in response to sudden-onset natural disasters in Bangladesh. Its facilitation of SRH, GBV, and A&Y coordination and programming has positioned UNFPA as a leader in these sectors. However, funding for these sectors remains consistently lower than other humanitarian sectors. This presents significant challenges to coverage of needs, particularly in disaster response. UNFPA has used and continues to diversify funding mechanisms to expand coverage, such as through IFI funding in Cox's Bazar."

EQ1: Relevance/Appropriateness
UNFPA mandate areas are integrated within the annual Joint Response Plans for the Rohingya refugee crisis as well as in national policy/planning and appeals documents for disaster response, including CERF appeals for anticipatory action funding.

EQ3: GBV Effectiveness

Rationale & Evidence
IFI Funding Innovation:
UNFPA previously worked with the World Bank on an integrated GBV and SRH project serving both refugees and host communities. New projects with the World Bank have a significant split (almost 50/50) of funding between Rohingya refugees and host communities.

EQ3: GBV Effectiveness
Uptake of GBV services in humanitarian crises in Bangladesh has improved, particularly in the refugee response, but also for disaster-affected populations. However, data on the impact of services on survivors, their families and communities are lacking.
Rationale & Evidence
Coverage Challenge:
While UNFPA meets its own coverage targets, the wider disaster response's ability to meet the overall coverage for people in need remains severely limited. One UNFPA key informant estimates coverage is about 15% to 20% "at best." UNFPA's target for SRH and GBV services relative to people in need was 7% in 2023.
Key Evidence:
"When we are talking about life saving intervention, and when there is an emergency, MISP is integrated as part of this SRH response. But when we are doing response, sometimes we feel it is undermined in terms of funding. Compared to other domains, like food security or Nutrition, SRH is very tiny amount. We are really able to reach a very teeny portion of the population." – UNFPA Key Informant

EQ5: Effectiveness/Leadership
For the refugee response, UNFPA has overall shown strong leadership in GBV, SRH, and youth coordination, building out capacity and partnership, and addressing challenges effectively as they arise.
Rationale & Evidence
Leadership Recognition:
In Cox's Bazar, UNFPA has taken a clear leadership role in GBV, SRH and youth coordination. UNFPA is a "very critical part of the overall coordination network" in the refugee response. The SRH WG launched the GoB Family Planning Strategy for Rohingya Refugees (2022-2025) with DGFP in 2022.
Funding Gap:
"The SRH thematic area was underfunded by almost 40% last year. If you look at the JRP trends, last year was better for UNFPA, because by the end of the year, we were 81% funded. But that hides the differences among the units. SRH remains consistently the most underfunded unit in UNFPA in Cox's Bazar." – UNFPA Staff
10
EQ5: Effectiveness/Leadership
For disaster responses, GBV coordination is widely recognized as effective. SRH coordination has faced more (external) challenges, but it is also functioning effectively. A&Y do not have a separate coordination mechanism; they utilize an integrated approach.
Rationale & Evidence
GBV Coordination:
Through its leadership of the GBV Cluster, in partnership with the MoWCA, UNFPA has had a "very strong voice" in establishing GBV coordination for disaster response. GBV protection issues were not widely considered important in disaster response before the introduction of the GBV Cluster in 2016.
SRH Coordination Challenge:
"Challenges existed with bringing attention to SRH in disasters because the Health Cluster sits under the Infectious Disease Control Department of MOH. Initially, SRH had not been prioritized by Health Cluster leadership because their orientation was towards disease outbreaks. In the past year, UNFPA had to do 'a bit of a heavy lifting…to make the SRH Working Group functional' within the Health Cluster, 'which is an achievement.'"

Suggested Action C
Country Level
UNFPA Bangladesh should consider ways in which it can improve several key areas of data collection and monitoring in emergencies, such as designing pre-disaster needs assessments directly with affected populations and developing more standardized approaches to illustrating the life-saving impact of GBV interventions. UNFPA Bangladesh should also update the MISP calculator for Bangladesh.

Linking to conclusion 3
Conclusion 3 ▼
"UNFPA plays a crucial role in data collection in humanitarian response, from assessments to targeted surveys to information management to programme monitoring, utilizing data to support the wider humanitarian community and to improve UNFPA's own programmatic response. However, several specific areas emerged for improvement in data collection and management for disaster preparedness and response. This includes pre-disaster consultations with affected populations, developing a common beneficiary database, and updating the MISP calculator. Data collection could also focus more strategically on capturing the impact of services, to better illustrate the life-saving nature of UNFPA-supported interventions, particularly related to GBV."

UNFPA is facilitating improved collection, use and dissemination of reliable programme and population data in humanitarian response in Bangladesh, although UNFPA could improve its ability to show how its humanitarian support is lifesaving for women and girls (particularly in the area of GBV), and generates important positive impacts for communities more broadly.
Rationale & Evidence
Corporate Database Need:
UNFPA, in collaboration with WFP, FAO, UNICEF and the Bangladesh Red Crescent Society, has developed a common beneficiary database for households most vulnerable to natural disasters. However, finalizing a UNFPA-specific database with data protection policies for use across country operations remains outstanding.
Key Evidence:
"UNFPA key informants felt it is important for UNFPA at the global level to develop a beneficiary database, accompanied by data protection policies and guidelines that can be utilized across country operations." "CO Bangladesh has been communicating to HQs on the need to create a corporate beneficiary database" and "HRD has not necessarily been proactive in this regard."

EQ7: Coherence
UNFPA harmonizes humanitarian response activities with sister agencies via a number of useful channels, although disaster response resolution on the common beneficiary database has impacted coordination on service delivery for AA.
Rationale & Evidence
Inter-Agency Database Challenge:
The challenges in finalizing a common beneficiary database across agencies has been identified as a barrier to a comprehensive and non-duplicative package of support. "UNFPA has had reservations due to the perceived sensitivity of its communities, which has resulted in some agencies perceiving UNFPA to be withholding data."
Key Evidence:
"Agencies have internal policies and data sharing agreements that prevent them from contributing to a common list. How to safely and ethically build out a common beneficiary database to facilitate anticipatory action and disaster response is an area the Country Office has consistently kept the regional office informed for support."

Suggested Action G
Global Level
UNFPA HRD should support the development of better monitoring systems for emergencies with a clear set of indicators. This monitoring should include strategies for collecting and sharing data on the life-saving impact of GBV programming (alongside SRH and A&Y programming).

Linking to conclusion 3
Conclusion 3 ▼
"UNFPA plays a crucial role in data collection in humanitarian response, from assessments to targeted surveys to information management to programme monitoring, utilizing data to support the wider humanitarian community and to improve UNFPA's own programmatic response. However, several specific areas emerged for improvement in data collection and management for disaster preparedness and response. This includes pre-disaster consultations with affected populations, developing a common beneficiary database, and updating the MISP calculator. Data collection could also focus more strategically on capturing the impact of services, to better illustrate the life-saving nature of UNFPA-supported interventions, particularly related to GBV."

EQ1: Relevance/Appropriateness
UNFPA is involved in and leads a variety of needs assessments that promote engagement of marginalized groups and support access to services for specific marginalized populations. However, an awareness of needs does not always translate into adequate response, particularly in disasters.
Rationale & Evidence
Assessment Gaps:
In disasters, UNFPA does not have "specific tools to assess people…rather we look at geographic location and district" immediately after a disaster. Several external key informants felt UNFPA should conduct more regular pre-disaster consultations and more directly involve vulnerable populations in preparedness planning.
Key Evidence:
"In one example, the lack of consultation with affected populations reportedly resulted in UNFPA distributing pre-packaged baby kits that were not felt to be appropriate to the setting." (Notably, these kits were adjusted through subsequent consultations with recipients.)

EQ4: Data for Response
UNFPA is facilitating improved collection, use and dissemination of reliable programme and population data in humanitarian response in Bangladesh, although UNFPA could improve its ability to show how its humanitarian support is lifesaving for women and girls (particularly in the area of GBV), and generates important positive impacts for communities more broadly.
Rationale & Evidence
Monitoring System Gap:
The lack of dedicated M&E staff for disaster response limits UNFPA capacity on data quality, consistency, timeliness, and disaggregation. Monitoring staff are temporarily appointed for 3 to 6 months. Several UNFPA key informants noted having a dedicated M&E focal point for disaster response is essential.
Key Evidence:
"The current tracking system for humanitarian response uses basic tools like google sheets to collect data, and a free version of KOBO collect, which is a challenge as this version limits how much data can be collected." "The absence of baseline or endline evaluations further inhibits the ability of UNFPA and its IPs to measure change."

Rationale & Evidence
Monitoring & Coordination:
UNFPA engages in joint information management initiatives, contributing to the 5W matrix and supporting collection, analysis, and sharing of data across sectors. UNFPA participates in joint monitoring visits, lessons learned workshops, and After-Action Reviews to assess effectiveness and improve future responses collectively.

Global Level
UNFPA should streamline its financial and administrative procedures to reduce delays for implementing partners in receiving funding, and develop strategies for more consistent engagement with IPs in project planning and financial management, including minimizing or eliminating pre-financing of activities by local partners.

Conclusion 5 ▼
"UNFPA has launched several procedural improvements and innovations at the CO level to facilitate humanitarian response, including developing its own SOPs for the CO's initial response to natural disaster triggering L1 and L2 in country-led response and drafting annual UNFPA-specific contingency plans for disaster response. Nevertheless, there are still some challenges with operational agility and accountability to IPs, particularly in terms of ensuring rapid disbursement of funds to partners in the early stages of disaster response."

EQ6: Efficiency
UNFPA Bangladesh has implemented several procedural improvements and innovations to facilitate humanitarian response, with key support from the UNFPA AP regional office.
Rationale & Evidence
SOPs & Innovations:
A significant achievement was the finalization in 2023 of country-level SOPs. According to a UNFPA key informant, their implementation has reduced response time significantly, from nearly three months after a disaster to three days. The introduction of decentralized procurement procedures in 2021 allows for quick mobilization to communities.
Key Evidence:
"The UNFPA CO has participated in and/or introduced a number of other innovations, such as those mentioned previously: AA, and the use of cash and voucher assistance leveraging blockchain technology for security." Several UNFPA key informants noted how useful the regional office has been in facilitating learning and providing support.
12
EQ6: Efficiency
Despite efficiency gains and innovation in humanitarian response, UNFPA Bangladesh still faces challenges with operational agility and partnerships.
Rationale & Evidence
Fund Disbursement Delays:
Challenges remain with timely resource mobilization, particularly the transfer of cash to IPs. Delays can be significant, sometimes 20-25 days or even a month for emergency funding. IPs expressed a desire for more reciprocal accountability and engagement, such as IPs having a stronger voice in programme design and adaptation.
Key Evidence:
"What is the ongoing challenge, because it's related with the programme implementation, is the fund disbursing ... sometimes consumes two or three months to pay under disbursing. So during this period, we need to continue our work from our fund, our own organizational fund." – UNFPA IP Key Informant. "In most of the cases, we are not involved in the design interface…it becomes a challenge for us, because sometimes the ground reality is different." – UNFPA IP Key Informant

EQ8: Connectedness
UNFPA promotes several humanitarian preparedness and response interventions in Bangladesh that support, and plan for, longer-term developmental and resilience-related goals.
Rationale & Evidence
Financial Procedure Gap for AA:
One area for improvement is addressing bottlenecks in funding to IPs for anticipatory action, so that IPs are not forced to 'pre-finance' initial costs. One UNFPA key informant recommended that UNFPA develop a specific global or regional-level fund and/or avail of money within HQ core resources from existing instruments (the ER, the HFT and the Emergency Response Reserve).
Key Evidence:
"We don't have any flexible policies and procedures supporting this system, like we had to distribute cash to the beneficiaries. But implementing partners supported us. They withdrew their own money to distribute cash because UNFPA was not able to transfer this cash to the IP at that quick time. So that financial procedure should be in place to support these kind of activities." – UNFPA Key Informant. "If [UNFPA] wants to stay relevant during disasters, then we need to have our own sort of substantive funds that we can quickly channelize. UNICEF has it, but we don't have it, and our mandate is equally important." – UNFPA Key Informant
